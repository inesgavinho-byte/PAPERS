# Method

## The sequence of the discipline

Every engagement follows the same order. None of the steps may be skipped, and none may be reversed.

**Observe.** The client brings symptoms, not necessarily the real problem. Understand reality before attempting to improve it.

**Understand.** Identify patterns. Separate symptoms from causes. Build a faithful model of the organisation.

**Architect.** Only after understanding reality: design the systems, governance, decision architecture, knowledge architecture and capabilities the organisation requires.

**Build.** Technology becomes implementation, not architecture. Architecture precedes construction.

**Evolve.** Reality changes. Observation continues. The architecture evolves. The organisation learns continuously.

## The operator

The first question the discipline asks of any object it examines:

**What is the invariant — what must remain true despite change?**

Applied to architecture, it answers what the architecture preserves. Applied to memory, what returns. Applied to evolution, what holds while form changes. Applied to identity, what the Ship of Theseus cannot carry away. The operator runs through the entire Canon; no analysis begins without it.

## The working method of the body of thought

Concepts are not written; they are discovered. The process is fixed:

**Dialogue → Canon → Paper.** Never the reverse.

Dialogues begin from a question, never from the destination. The initial hypothesis must be allowed to die in the course of the dialogue — as *decision*, *production* and *continuity* died. A dialogue that opens already convinced of its answer has stopped discovering and started confirming; that is the moment the Canon would cease to be an instrument of inquiry and become a system of confirmation. The method is protected only by keeping the hypothesis mortal to the end.

A dialogue examines a question until a definition survives the four tests. The definition enters the Canon. Papers are then written as applications of the Canon — which is why, if the definitions are genuinely new and consistent, the papers nearly write themselves.

No Paper may introduce a concept the Canon does not contain. When a Paper needs a concept that does not yet exist, writing stops and a dialogue opens.

## Two kinds of document

The discipline produces two kinds of document, and they must not be confused.

**Foundation documents** *discover*: the Canon, the Glossary, the Axioms, the Method, the Boundaries, the Dialogues. They establish what is true within the discipline and how its words are fixed.

**Derived documents** *explain*: Papers, white papers, books, lectures, editorial diagrams, and implementations such as DECIMA and the platform. They apply the foundation; they do not add to it.

The direction is one-way. A derived document may cite, use and dramatise the foundation, but may never silently amend it — if writing a Paper reveals that a definition is wrong or missing, the Paper stops and the correction happens in a dialogue, not in the Paper. This is what keeps the body of work coherent as it grows: fifty Papers, one foundation.

## Three conceptual layers

The publication operates across three distinct layers:

**Canon terms** define concepts. They state what a term means inside the discipline.

**Ideas** organise inquiry. They are editorial territories that gather Papers across time.

**Papers** make arguments. Each Paper develops one thesis within one or more Ideas, grounded in the Canon.

A term may exist in more than one layer, but its function changes. In the Canon, *Pattern* is defined. As an Idea, *Pattern* gathers Papers that explore it as a territory of inquiry. The layers do not conflict because they do different work — one fixes meaning, one organises reading, one argues.

Two consequences follow for the choice of Ideas. First, an Idea earns its place by *dividing* the corpus, not mirroring it: a territory that would gather nearly every Paper (as an axis-term like *Pattern* or *Organisation* would) organises nothing. Ideas are the terms that separate Papers into genuine territories — Observation, Judgement, Memory, Knowledge. Second, an axis-term may still become an Idea when it reveals a real continuity rather than a universal tag — *Pattern* enters as an Idea only where it discloses that two Papers, or two Volumes, were the same inquiry all along. Used so, it divides by revealing; used as a blanket tag, it would absorb everything. Parsimony is the rule.
