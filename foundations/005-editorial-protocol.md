# Editorial Protocol

How PAPERS is written, and why no published text may read as machine-made.

PAPERS exists to build a body of thought intended to remain relevant for decades. A text that reads as generated is a text that reads as replaceable, and nothing replaceable belongs in this publication. This protocol is binding on every Paper, without exception.

## 1. Authorship

Large language models are never authors. They may assist with critique, structure, contradiction-finding, compression, editing and consistency checks. They must not originate the intellectual thesis. The role of the model is editor, critic and structural assistant — never ghostwriter.

## 2. Origin

The first idea always comes from Inês Gavinho. Every Paper begins from a human observation, question, discomfort, contradiction or lived pattern. A Paper that cannot name its originating observation has no origin, and a text without origin has no author.

## 3. The research trail

No Paper is published without a private research trail:

- raw notes;
- a first-principles draft;
- counterarguments;
- references or genealogy;
- rejected formulations;
- the final editorial draft.

The trail is not bureaucracy. It is the evidence that thinking occurred before writing did.

## 4. Conceptual risk

No Paper may be published unless it contains a real conceptual risk: a distinction, thesis or definition that an intelligent reader could challenge. A text that risks nothing asserts nothing.

## 5. Prohibited patterns

The recognisable habits of machine prose are excluded:

- generic summaries;
- symmetrical list-making without necessity;
- over-explaining the obvious;
- motivational phrasing;
- inflated claims;
- predictable paragraph rhythm;
- the repetition of "not only X, but Y";
- conclusions that restate the introduction;
- empty phrases of the kind "in today's rapidly changing world".

Where one of these appears in a draft, it is removed — not softened.

## 6. Thought over fluency

PAPERS privileges thought over fluency. A slightly difficult, precise sentence is preferable to a smooth generic one. Smoothness is not a virtue here; it is a symptom.

## 7. Authorial voice

Every text is revised for voice before publication: sober, precise, essayistic, intellectually rigorous, in British English. No marketing language. No self-promotion. No forced accessibility.

## 8. The five tests

Each Paper must pass five editorial tests before publication:

1. Does it originate from a real observation?
2. Does it introduce a necessary conceptual distinction?
3. Does the thesis create intellectual tension?
4. Could this have been written without knowing Inês Gavinho's body of thought?
5. Will it still be worth reading in ten years?

The fourth test is answered correctly only in the negative.

## 9. Failure

If the answer to any test is weak, the Paper returns to research. It is not polished. Polish conceals weakness; research removes it.

## 10. Precedence

Where this protocol conflicts with convenience, deadline or enthusiasm, the protocol prevails.
