# Dialogue 001 — What is an Organisation?

**Status: closed. Both interlocutors declared the definition cannot be improved without losing explanatory power. Entered the Canon.**

This document records the derivation of the first Canon entry. It preserves the rejected formulations and the reasons for their rejection, so that in ten years the choice can be understood, not merely known.

---

## Amendment (from Dialogue 004)

The identity clause — *its identity is preserved not by its members but by its architecture* — was found to sit one level below the true mechanism. A university replaces its buildings, software, structure and rector — it changes its architecture — and remains the same university, because its **pattern** is preserved. What preserves identity is therefore the pattern; architecture is the means.

The clause was right in direction (not the members) and wrong in level (architecture is the means, not the cause). Corrected, separating the duality across its two rungs:

> *Its identity is preserved by its constitutive pattern; its architecture is how that pattern is kept.*

The Canon correcting itself one level deeper is the system working, not failing. The full Organisation entry carries the corrected wording once Pattern closes in Dialogue 004.

## The question

Every Paper in preparation assumes we know what an organisation is. It had never been defined. An architect defines the object before redesigning it.

## Candidate 1 — decision-centred

> *An organisation is a system for producing decisions beyond the capacity of a single mind — and for preserving what those decisions teach.*

**Attack:** does a university exist to produce decisions? A hospital? A church? No. Decisions are a means, not the end.

**Verdict:** rejected. The mechanism had usurped the centre.

## Candidate 2 — coordination-centred

> *An organisation is a living system that coordinates people, knowledge and resources towards a shared purpose across time.*

**Attack:** true, but not distinctive — it is what the management literature has said for sixty years. A definition that does no work the current definitions do not already do fails Test 2.

**Verdict:** rejected as sufficient; retained as the first sentence. Purpose and continuity stay at the centre; decision and learning return as the metabolism that sustains them.

## Candidate 3 — the membership criterion

> *...Its identity survives the replacement of its members...*

Introduced to exclude the family (whose members are its identity) and to ground the axiom *systems outlive people*.

**Attack (Ship of Theseus):** in a two-partner firm, one partner leaves and is replaced — the same organisation? Both founders leave while brand, people and clients continue — the same organisation? The criterion is ambiguous exactly where the discipline will first operate: small organisations.

**Resolution:** what survives is not the membership; it is the architecture. The criterion becomes a mechanism:

> *Its identity is preserved not by its members but by its architecture.*

This converts a criterion into a consequence and links directly to the principle that every organisation is already an architecture.

**Circularity check:** if *organisation* is defined through *architecture*, and *architecture* is later defined as the structures of an organisation, the system is circular. Resolved by defining architecture over **systems**: *the structure through which a system observes, remembers, decides and learns.* The chain is then clean: system → living system → organisation, with architecture as a system-level concept.

**Genealogical note:** in the theory of living systems (Maturana and Varela; Beer), *organisation* names precisely this — the invariant pattern of relations that persists while the components change. The correction is aligned with half a century of systems theory.

## On "living"

Challenged as decoration. Defended as premise: a living system must continuously produce its own continuity — it degrades if it stops maintaining itself. A bridge is a system; it is not alive. The second sentence of the definition (*its continuity depends on...*) only makes sense if the system is alive.

**Consequence accepted:** if organisations are living, they can sicken, adapt, degenerate and die. This changes the language of the discipline and opens organisational pathology as a future territory. Test 4 satisfied.

**Obligation created:** *living system* must be defined in the Glossary before further use.

## Boundary cases tested

- **Market** — no shared purpose, only aggregated individual purposes. Excluded.
- **Crowd / spontaneous movement** — no continuity dependent on deciding and learning. Excluded.
- **Project** — exists in order to end. Excluded by *across time*.
- **Family** — members are the identity. Excluded by the architecture clause.

## Current candidate

> *An organisation is a living system that coordinates people, knowledge and resources towards a shared purpose across time. Its identity is preserved not by its members but by its architecture; its continuity depends on the system's capacity to decide and to learn — which is why an organisation endures not by what it owns, but by what it can continue to understand.*

**Honest note:** the final clause (*endures not by what it owns...*) is rhetoric, not a falsifiable claim. It is retained knowingly — a Canon also teaches how to think, not only how to classify.

## Closure condition

The entry closes when both interlocutors declare: *it cannot be improved without losing explanatory power.* Not yet declared.

## Derived provisional definitions

This dialogue produced, in passing: System (outcomes, not behaviour) · Architecture (over systems; deliberateness excluded) · Enterprise (sustains its purpose through economic value) · Company (a legal form through which an enterprise operates). Recorded in the Glossary as provisional.
