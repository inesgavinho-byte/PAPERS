The greatest failure of an organisation is not bankruptcy. It is forgetting.

A company can lose money and recover. It can lose a market and find another. What it cannot easily recover from is the quiet erosion of the reasons behind its own decisions — the slow disappearance of the judgement that once made it coherent.

## Founders are a form of memory

In the early life of a company, the founder *is* the institution's memory. They hold, without writing anything down, the answers to the questions that matter: why this client and not that one, why this standard, why we refuse certain work even when it pays. The organisation runs on a memory that lives in a single mind.

This works beautifully and dangerously. Beautifully, because judgement is fast and consistent. Dangerously, because it is undocumented and mortal.

> An organisation that depends on a founder for its judgement has not yet become an organisation. It is a person with employees.

## The transition no one schedules

There is a moment — rarely marked, almost never planned — when a company must decide whether its memory will continue to live in people or whether it will become explicit. Most companies do not decide. They drift. The founder steps back, the originating logic fades, and what remains is a set of habits whose reasons no one can reconstruct.

The work, then, is not succession of *roles*. It is succession of *understanding*.

## What it means to outlive

To outlive a founder is not to preserve them. It is to make the institution capable of producing, on its own, the kind of judgement the founder once produced alone — and to improve on it.

That requires three things:

- **Explicit reasoning.** Decisions recorded with their *why*, not only their *what*.
- **Transferable standards.** Quality defined so it can be taught, not merely demonstrated.
- **Distributed memory.** Knowledge held in relationships and systems, not in one indispensable person.

A company that achieves this does something rare. It converts a person into an institution — and an institution, unlike a person, can choose to remember.

That is the real ambition. Not permanence for its own sake, but the continuation of value beyond the moment that created it.
